# Patent Plate

A design philosophy for objects that remember being drawn by hand before they were built.

Space is organised the way an engineer's plate is organised: one subject, drawn true to scale, sitting on a single ground line that divides the sheet. Everything above that line is atmosphere, dark and deep, where the subject is lit like an object on a workbench at night. Everything below is the record: pale paper, measured, annotated and quietly certain. The line between them is the most important mark on the page, and it is placed with painstaking attention, because the whole composition rests on it.

Colour is material rather than decoration. Charcoal is graphite and ink; cream and stone are drafting paper; brass is the instrument, used only where something is measured or named; burgundy is the one moment of warmth, the lacquer on a single panel, reserved for the invitation. No gradients, no shadows, no texture added for effect. Every flat field is meticulously crafted so that its edges are exact and its proportions feel inevitable.

Scale carries the hierarchy. One element is enormous and everything else is small, precise and patient. Dimension lines, figure numbers and tick marks are drawn with the restraint of a master draughtsman: thin, sparse and always true. They describe what is real and nothing more. Repetition appears only where the subject repeats itself, such as two wheels, one rhythm.

Typography behaves like lettering on a technical drawing that has grown up. A serif with the weight of cast metal names the subject; a clean grotesque does the labelling. Words are few, set with generous space and aligned to one grid, so that the eye travels the page in the order a careful reader would choose. Nothing touches, nothing crowds, and nothing falls off the edge; the result should read as the product of deep expertise and countless hours of refinement.

The finished sheet should feel like an artefact: a plate removed from a bound folio, kept because it is beautiful as well as correct. It rewards a second look. Its craftsmanship is master-level throughout, so that the invitation it carries is believed.
